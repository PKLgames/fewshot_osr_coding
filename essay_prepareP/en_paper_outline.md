## I. Introduction
**[Word budget: ~800 words | No tables/figures | ~0.9 page]**

**[Word budget: ~800 words total]**
- Problem definition + motivation paragraph: ~150 words
- Core challenges (3 items, brief elaboration): ~200 words
- Contributions (3 items, each with 1-2 supporting sentences): ~300 words
- Paper organization / roadmap sentence: ~150 words

**Problem Definition:** Few-Shot Open-Set Audio Classification (FOAC) — the dual task of simultaneously classifying known-class query samples and rejecting unknown-class samples, given only a small number of labeled support examples (1–10 shots per class).

**Core Challenges:**
1. Class prototype estimation is inherently unreliable under few-shot conditions [1], [2].
2. Open-set scenarios demand explicit known/unknown discrimination; conventional closed-set classifiers produce overconfident predictions on unknown classes [5], [6].
3. Generalization degrades under domain shift, both within-domain (acoustic scene variations) and cross-domain (environmental sound → speech) [19], [20].

<!-- INSERT: essay_metirial/fig1_intro_foac_problem.png  (generated by cont1_intro_figure.py) -->
<!--        FOAC problem conceptual diagram — known/unknown separation, three core challenges -->

**Contributions (three-fold):**
1. We propose a unified FOAC framework based on episodic training [1], [2], integrating six complementary open-set detection heads — anti-prototype [9], Mahalanobis distance [8], OSR23, Extended V2, Cluster V2, and Fusion V2 — trained end-to-end with a multi-objective loss.
2. We design the Fusion V2 detection head, which fuses the discriminative boundary of the extended head with the generative density estimation of the cluster head. On the TAU19 dataset [19] under 6-way 5-shot, Fusion V2 achieves AUROC = 0.985 and OSR Score = 93.31%, outperforming the anti-prototype baseline [9] by 25–35 percentage points.
3. We establish a comprehensive evidence chain for FOAC evaluation through systematic way-shot scaling, openness analysis, cross-domain generalization testing, component ablation, and statistical significance testing (Friedman + Nemenyi) [12].

**[~300 words]**
- Conventional approaches: SoftMax thresholding [5], OpenMax [6], G-OpenMax [7].
- Distance- and density-based methods: Mahalanobis distance [8], Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) [24].

- Critical gap: existing OSR methods assume abundant training samples; performance degrades severely in the few-shot regime.

## III. Method
**[Word budget: ~1,800 words total | TABLE 1 + Fig 1 | ~2.5 pages]**

### A. Problem Formulation
**[~300 words | 2-3 equations]**
- **Base classes** (D_base): large-scale labeled data for training the feature extractor.
- **Novel classes** (D_novel): N-way K-shot evaluation episodes.
- **Unknown classes**: unseen categories appearing at test time, to be rejected.
- **Formalization**: given a support set S (N classes × K samples per class), for each query sample x, output a classification distribution over known classes and an open-set score for unknown rejection.

### B. Episodic Training Framework
**[~350 words | Fig 1: architecture overview]**
- **Episode construction**: each episode randomly samples N-way × (K support + Q query) examples, mixing known and unknown class samples [1], [2].
- **Backbone**: YAMNet [11] pretrained on AudioSet [10] (frozen or optionally fine-tuned).
- **Flow Transform**: an optional feature-space normalizing flow mapping [17], [18] (adds ~25K parameters).

### C. Six OSR Detection Heads
**[~700 words total | Fig 1 (six heads within dashed box)]**
- Each head: ~100-120 words describing mechanism + key equation

**1. Anti-Prototype Head** (anti_proto) **[~110 words]**
- Learns an "anti-prototype" serving as the negative center of all known classes [9].
- Open-set score = distance to the anti-prototype vs. distance to the nearest class prototype.

**2. Mahalanobis Distance Head** (mahal) **[~100 words]**
- Fits per-class Gaussian distributions in the feature space [8].
- Open-set score = Mahalanobis distance to the nearest class centroid.

**3. OOD Head OSR23** (osr23) **[~100 words]**
- Trains a GMM to model the known-class feature distribution [24].
- Open-set score = GMM-based log-likelihood ratio.

**4. OOD Head Extended V2** (ext_v2) **[~100 words]**
- Extended OOD detection head with a learnable transformation layer [8], [24].
- Open-set score = learned discriminative boundary.

**5. OOD Head Cluster V2** (clust_v2) **[~100 words]**
- Cluster-based open-set detection [24].
- Known-class features form clusters; open-set score = distance to the nearest cluster centroid.

**6. OOD Head Fusion V2** (fusion_v2) — **Core Contribution** **[~200 words | 1 key equation + fusion schematic in Fig 1]**
- Fuses the outputs of Extended V2 and Cluster V2.
- Weighted combination with learnable fusion parameters.
- Open-set score = α · score_ext_v2 + (1 − α) · score_clust_v2.
- Training objective: L = L_classification + λ_osr · L_osr + λ_reciprocal · L_reciprocal.

### D. Training Objectives
**[~250 words | 2-3 key loss equations]**
- **Classification Loss**: cross-entropy on support + query known classes.
- **OSR Loss**: binary cross-entropy (known vs. unknown) for training the open-set detection heads [5], [6].
- **Reciprocal Loss**: reciprocal attention regularization (weight = 0.3) [9].
- **Separation Loss**: feature separation loss between known and unknown classes (weight = 0.02) [24].
- **Gradient Clipping**: global gradient clipping for training stability.

Note: TAU19 is the more challenging domain (greater distribution shift across recording devices and acoustic environments) [19].

**Evaluation Settings:**
- Default: 6-way 5-shot, Q = 15 few-shot query samples.
- Way-Shot scan: 2–6 way × 1–10 shot.
- Openness analysis: 2–4 unknown classes.
- Cross-domain: TAU22 ↔ TAU19 bidirectional.

**Metrics:**
- Classification: Accuracy (ACC, %), F1-score, Classification Macro-AUROC (one-vs-rest).
- Open-set: AUROC [14], OSR Score = (TPR + TNR) / 2 at TNR = 95%, TPR@TNR95.
- Efficiency: Number of Parameters (NP), Average Inference Time (AIT, ms).

**Calibration Protocol:**
- Per-round recalibration: the threshold is recalibrated for each evaluation round to achieve TNR = 95% on the calibration split.
- Calibration set ≠ Test set, preventing the self-calibration feedback loop that produces degenerate constant-TNR results.

**Hardware and Hyperparameters:**
- GPU: NVIDIA RTX 3090.
- Training episodes: 3,000 (full) / 500 (quick validation).
- Optimizer: AdamW [13], learning rate = 2 × 10⁻⁴, warmup = 200 steps.
- Backbone: YAMNet [11] (AudioSet [10]-pretrained; ~12.7K trainable parameters in the baseline configuration).

**Key Findings:**
1. Cross-domain OSR performance is highly asymmetric: TAU22 → TAU19 (OSR Score = 90.49%, AUROC = 0.977) substantially exceeds TAU19 → TAU22 (OSR Score = 74.31%, AUROC = 0.878). When TAU19 serves as the target domain, its clearer open-space boundary facilitates unknown detection.
2. Fusion V2 is the best method in both directions: on TAU22 → TAU19, it leads anti_prototype by +37.7 pp OSR Score and +0.302 AUROC; on TAU19 → TAU22, it leads mahalanobis by +23.4 pp OSR Score and +0.253 AUROC.
3. The OOD head family (osr23, ext_v2, clust_v2) exhibits highly consistent performance under cross-domain evaluation (OSR Score ≈ 84%, AUROC ≈ 0.93 on TAU22 → TAU19), while fusion_v2 further improves by ~6 pp.
4. On TAU19 → TAU22, all methods degrade by approximately 10 pp OSR Score (84% → 74%), underscoring the substantial impact of cross-domain direction.
5. Classification accuracy drops sharply under domain shift (Base 5s TAU22 → TAU19: 52.27% → 35.13%), yet OSR capability paradoxically improves (76.96% → 90.49%), suggesting that classification and open-set detection rely on distinct feature properties.

## V. Discussion
**[Word budget: ~850 words total | No tables/figures | ~0.9 page]**

### A. Why Fusion V2 Works
**[~180 words]**
<!-- INSERT: essay_metirial/fig5_1_fusion_complementarity.png  (generated by cont5_discussion_figures.py) -->
- Fusion V2 combines two complementary signals: the discriminative boundary from the extended head [8] and the generative density estimation from the cluster head [24].
- Discriminative methods excel at modeling known-class boundaries; generative methods are sensitive to distant unknown samples.
- The fusion advantage is larger when the known/unknown boundary is well-separated (TAU19: AUROC margin +0.04–0.05) than when it is ambiguous (TAU22: margin +0.02–0.04).
- AUROC provides greater discriminative resolution than OSR Score [14]: TAU19 ablation AUROC spread = 0.354 vs. OSR Score spread = 40.5 pp. As a threshold-free metric, AUROC is more stable and less sensitive to calibration variance.

### B. The wo_ood_head Surprise
**[~180 words]**
- Removing the OOD-specific training loss paradoxically improves OSR performance: TAU19 wo_ood_head fusion_v2 AUROC = 0.985 (global optimum across all experiments).
- Hypothesis: the OOD loss introduces noisy gradients that interfere with natural feature-space separation; the combination of classification loss and reciprocal attention [9] alone is sufficient to induce effective known/unknown separation.
- Practical implication: when feature quality is sufficiently high, a simple OSR head with good features outperforms complex OSR-specific training objectives [5], [6].
- This effect is less pronounced on TAU22 (wo_ood_head AUROC = 0.872 vs. full_model = 0.862), suggesting that easier domains are less sensitive to training regularization.

### C. Cross-Domain Asymmetry
**[~150 words]**
<!-- INSERT: essay_metirial/fig5_3_cross_domain_asymmetry.png  (generated by cont5_discussion_figures.py) -->
- TAU22 → TAU19 OSR Score (90.49%) substantially exceeds TAU19 → TAU22 (74.31%), a gap of 16.2 pp. Similarly, AUROC: 0.977 vs. 0.878, a gap of 0.099.
- Interpretation: TAU19 [19] source-domain samples exhibit greater acoustic diversity, leading the model to learn a wider open-space boundary, which in turn confers greater robustness to unknown classes.
- Practical implication: training data should span diverse acoustic conditions to maximize cross-domain OSR generalization.

### D. AUROC vs. OSR Score as Evaluation Metrics
**[~150 words]**
<!-- INSERT: essay_metirial/fig5_2_auroc_vs_osr_score.png  (generated by cont5_discussion_figures.py) -->
- AUROC exhibits higher discriminative resolution [14]: in the ablation matrix, the AUROC spread substantially exceeds the OSR Score spread.
- OSR Score depends on TNR = 95% threshold calibration; per-round recalibration introduces additional variance.
- The three OOD head family methods (osr23, ext_v2, clust_v2) are nearly indistinguishable under OSR Score (<1 pp difference) but show subtle differentiation under AUROC.
- **Recommendation**: adopt AUROC as the primary OSR evaluation metric and report OSR Score as a supplementary measure.

### E. Limitations
**[~190 words | 5 items, each 1-2 sentences]**
1. Only two within-domain acoustic scene datasets (TAU22 [20], TAU19 [19]) are evaluated; truly cross-domain datasets (speech, musical instruments, e.g., NSynth [30], LibriSpeech [31]) are needed for broader validation.
2. The Fusion V2 blending weight α requires manual tuning; future work should investigate jointly learned fusion parameters.
3. The YAMNet backbone [11], pretrained on AudioSet [10], may introduce known-class data leakage (TAU22/TAU19 acoustic scene classes may appear in AudioSet).
4. No comparison with recent foundation models (CLAP [15], AST [16], PaSST [28], etc.) has been conducted.
5. The diminishing-returns phenomenon of AUROC with increasing shots in the way-shot experiments currently lacks a theoretical explanation.

## VI. Conclusion
**[Word budget: ~350 words | No tables/figures | ~0.4 page]**
- 总结贡献: ~200 words
- 未来方向: ~150 words

1. We proposed and systematically evaluated six OSR detection heads within an episodic FOAC framework. **Fusion V2 is the best method across all experimental settings** (12/12 way-shot, 6/6 ablation configurations, 4/4 openness conditions, 2/2 cross-domain directions), with highly significant statistical validation (Friedman χ² = 49.29, p = 1.94 × 10⁻⁹) [12].
2. Fusion V2 core results: TAU19 wo_ood_head AUROC = **0.985** (global optimum); TAU22 wo_flow_transform AUROC = **0.884** (TAU22 optimum); TAU19 openness AUROC maintained at **0.984–0.992**.
3. AUROC, as a threshold-free metric [14], provides stronger discriminative power than OSR Score: ablation matrix AUROC spread = 0.354 vs. OSR Score spread = 40.5 pp, and is robust to threshold calibration noise.
4. A counterintuitive finding: removing the OOD-specific training loss (wo_ood_head) yields the global AUROC optimum of 0.985 on TAU19, suggesting that *simple OSR head + good features* can outperform *complex OSR training objectives*.
5. Future directions: lightweight deployment (removing Flow Transform [17], [18] yields 6.5× speedup and −63% parameters), validation on a third cross-domain dataset (e.g., NSynth [30], LibriSpeech [31]), comparison with foundation models (CLAP [15] / AST [16] / PaSST [28]), and jointly learned fusion weights.
