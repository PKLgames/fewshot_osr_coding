## V. Discussion

### A. Why Fusion V2 Works

Fusion V2 combines complementary signals. The extended head [8] learns a discriminative boundary—accurate when test unknowns resemble training, brittle otherwise. The cluster head [24] scores by proximity to support points—less sensitive to distribution shift but noisier at small K. When one is weak, the other compensates.

The data reflect this. On TAU19, where per-device modes create clean inter-cluster gaps, the fusion margin reaches +0.035–0.050 AUROC; on TAU22, where uniform recording blurs boundaries, only +0.021. The learned weight \(\alpha\) tilts toward discriminative when boundaries are sharp and toward density otherwise—explaining fusion_v2's clean sweep across all conditions. AUROC spread across TAU19 ablation is 0.354 vs. OSR Score spread of 40.5 pp; as a threshold-free metric [14], AUROC captures the full ranking without per-round calibration variance (§V.D).

### B. The wo_ood_head Surprise

The global optimum is wo_ood_head + fusion_v2 on TAU19: AUROC = 0.985. Removing the OSR loss—designed to teach known/unknown separation—improves open-set detection.

We hypothesize gradient interference: early in training, the OSR loss pushes \(s_{\text{osr}}\) in directions conflicting with the classification gradient. Reciprocal attention [9] and separation losses supply a cleaner separation signal; with a strong pretrained backbone [11], OSR supervision adds noise. The effect is asymmetric: wo_ood_head gains +0.010 AUROC on TAU22 (0.872 vs. 0.862) but +0.013 on TAU19 (0.985 vs. 0.972)—the harder dataset benefits more.

The practical moral: with a strong pretrained backbone, a well-chosen head and clean classification loss beat carefully engineered multi-term objectives [5], [6]. Good features beat complicated losses.

### C. Cross-Domain Asymmetry

TAU22→TAU19 OSR Score = 90.49% vs. TAU19→TAU22 = 74.31%—a 16.2 pp gap (AUROC: 0.977 vs. 0.878). The cause is target-domain geometry: TAU19 [19] spans three devices with wide quality range, producing well-separated per-device clusters with clean gaps where unknowns land. TAU22's uniform recordings produce tighter, overlapping clusters with less open space.

Implication: train on the acoustically more diverse dataset—even at the cost of within-domain accuracy—since wider feature distributions act as implicit open-set regularization. Deliberate augmentation with multi-device recordings to widen the known-class distribution is an unexplored direction.

### D. AUROC vs. OSR Score as Evaluation Metrics

The choice of primary metric shapes conclusions. AUROC discriminates methods better than OSR Score: the three OOD head methods differ by < 1 pp in OSR Score but show systematic AUROC gaps of 0.005–0.010. OSR Score is a point estimate at TNR = 95% with calibration variance; AUROC [14], a threshold-free integral, averages this out.

Under OSR Score, osr23, ext_v2, clust_v2 appear interchangeable. Under AUROC, osr23 ≈ clust_v2 > ext_v2 is consistently visible and statistically confirmed (§IV.C). We recommend AUROC as the primary FOAC open-set metric, with OSR Score and TPR@TNR95 as supplementary.

### E. Limitations

First, we evaluate two datasets from one domain family (DCASE); they share class semantics and spectrotemporal statistics. Validation on cross-domain data—speech [31], musical instruments [30], bioacoustics—is needed.

Second, the fusion weight \(\alpha\) is a single scalar. Per-episode weights conditioned on the support set could adapt to episode-level boundary variation.

Third, YAMNet [11] was pretrained on AudioSet [10], whose categories may overlap with TAU19/TAU22, potentially inflating metrics. A disjoint-data backbone study would quantify this.

Fourth, we have not compared against foundation models (CLAP [15], AST [16], PaSST [28]); whether the wo_ood_head finding holds under stronger representations is unknown.

Fifth, AUROC near-saturation with increasing shots (§IV.G1) lacks theory: K = 1 → 10 gains 10–15 pp ACC but under 0.02 AUROC. Modeling the relation between prototype estimation error and open-set score variance would clarify whether this is fundamental or architectural.
