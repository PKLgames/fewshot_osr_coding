Few-shot open-set audio classification requires a model to recognize known sound categories from sparse labeled examples while simultaneously rejecting queries from unseen classes—a dual demand that neither few-shot learning nor open-set recognition alone addresses. This paper proposes a unified episodic training framework integrating six complementary open-set recognition detection heads: anti-prototype, Mahalanobis distance, OSR23, Extended V2, Cluster V2, and Fusion V2. Fusion V2, the core contribution, combines a discriminative boundary signal with a generative density estimate through a learned scalar weight. Evaluated on the DCASE TAU19 and TAU22 acoustic scene datasets, Fusion V2 achieves an AUROC of 0.985 on TAU19 (global optimum) and 0.884 on TAU22, outperforming all baselines across every experimental condition—12 way-shot settings, 6 ablation configurations, 4 openness levels, and bidirectional cross-domain transfer. Statistical significance is confirmed via the Friedman test with Nemenyi post-hoc analysis (p < 10⁻⁸). A counterintuitive finding emerges: removing the open-set-specific training loss yields the best overall performance, suggesting that strong pretrained features with a well-structured detection head can outperform complex multi-objective training recipes. The framework supports lightweight deployment: removing the flow transform cuts inference time by a factor of 6.5 while preserving competitive detection accuracy.

Keywords: Few-shot learning, open-set recognition, audio classification, episodic training, acoustic scene classification, out-of-distribution detection.
