## I. Introduction

Few-shot open-set audio classification (FOAC) sits at the intersection of few-shot learning [1], [2], [3] and open-set recognition [5], [6], [9]: given only 1–10 labeled support examples per class, a model must simultaneously classify queries from known categories and reject queries from unknown categories unseen during training. In real-world acoustic monitoring, these two challenges are inseparable—a deployed system must recognize pre-defined sound classes from sparse annotations while flagging novel acoustic events outside its known taxonomy. Conventional closed-set classifiers produce overconfident predictions on unfamiliar classes [5], [6], rendering them unfit for open-world deployment.

The FOAC task is compounded by three core difficulties. First, class prototype estimation is inherently unreliable under few-shot conditions. Prototypical networks [1] average support embeddings to form class representations; with only one to five samples per class, these estimates exhibit high variance that degrades both classification accuracy and distance-based open-set detection [8]. Second, open-set discrimination demands an explicit known/unknown separation mechanism: closed-set classifiers map all inputs onto the known-class simplex and cannot express uncertainty about unfamiliar categories [6]. Third, generalization degrades under domain shift. Acoustic scene characteristics vary substantially across recording devices, environments, and locations [19], [20]; a model trained on one dataset may encounter fundamentally different feature distributions when deployed in another domain—for instance, from environmental sound to speech [25], [31]—and this mismatch amplifies the difficulty of both sub-tasks.

To address these challenges, we propose a unified episodic training framework [1], [2] integrating six complementary open-set recognition (OSR) detection heads within a single end-to-end architecture: anti-prototype learning [9] (negative class center), Mahalanobis distance [8] (per-class Gaussians), OSR23 (Gaussian mixture models [24]), Extended V2 (discriminative boundary transformation [8]), Cluster V2 (cluster-based density estimation [24]), and Fusion V2—our core contribution—which fuses the discriminative boundary of the extended head with the generative density estimate of the cluster head via a learnable weighted combination. All heads are trained jointly with a multi-objective loss: classification cross-entropy, open-set binary cross-entropy [5], reciprocal attention regularization [9], and feature separation [24].

Our contributions are three-fold. First, we conduct the first systematic comparison of six OSR detection paradigms under a unified FOAC framework. On TAU19 [19] under 6-way 5-shot, Fusion V2 achieves AUROC = 0.985 and OSR Score = 93.31%, outperforming the anti-prototype baseline [9] by over 30 pp and the next-best head by ~0.05 AUROC. Second, we establish a comprehensive evidence chain spanning five dimensions: way-shot scaling, openness analysis, bidirectional cross-domain generalization [19], [20], component-level ablation (6 configs × 6 heads = 36 conditions), and statistical significance testing (Friedman + Nemenyi [12], p = 1.94 × 10⁻⁹). This demonstrates that OSR head choice is far more consequential than architectural details, AUROC provides stronger discriminative resolution than OSR Score [14], and fusion yields the greatest benefit in challenging domains. Third, we report a counterintuitive finding: removing the OOD-specific training loss yields the global AUROC optimum of 0.985 on TAU19, suggesting that with sufficiently expressive features, a simple head with strong representations can outperform complex OSR-specific objectives [5], [6].
