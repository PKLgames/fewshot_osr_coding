## IV. Experiments

### A. Experimental Datasets and Setup

We evaluate on two DCASE acoustic scene classification benchmarks, summarized in TABLE 2.

**TAU22** [20] (DCASE 2022 Task 1) provides 6 base classes (airport, bus, metro, park, public square, shopping mall) and 4 novel classes (street pedestrian, tram, urban park, pedestrian street), recorded under controlled conditions with consistent microphone placement. The corpus contains ~10K one-second clips, split 70/30 between base and novel.

**TAU19** [19] (DCASE 2019 Task 1a) shares the same class semantics but was collected across three devices (A: high-quality binaural; B, C: consumer smartphones) with substantially different microphone quality, producing intra-class variance 1.5–2× larger than TAU22. TAU19 is harder for classification, but its well-separated per-device acoustic modes create a sharper known/unknown boundary that benefits open-set detection.

**[Insert TABLE 2 about here.]**

Audio is preprocessed to 64-band log-mel spectrograms (96 frames, 0.96 s) and encoded by frozen YAMNet [10], [11] (1024-dim). Default protocol: 6-way 5-shot, Q = 15 queries per known class, M unknowns drawn uniformly from the unknown pool. Training: 3,000 episodes, AdamW [13] (\(lr = 2 \times 10^{-4}\), 200 warmup steps), NVIDIA RTX 3090. Each episode is independently sampled (~90,000 unique support configurations). Cross-domain (§IV.E) uses 200 epochs.

We report five metrics. Closed-set: accuracy (ACC, %) and classification macro-AUROC (Cls-AUROC, one-vs-rest). Open-set: AUROC [14] (primary, see §V.D), OSR Score = (TPR + TNR) / 2 at TNR = 95%, and TPR@TNR95. The rejection threshold \(\tau\) is calibrated per round on a held-out 50% support split disjoint from prototypes and test queries, avoiding the self-calibration loop. Inference time (AIT) and parameter count are in §IV.D.

### B. Comparison of OSR Detection Methods

We compare all six OSR heads under the default FOAC setting (6-way 5-shot, full_model, 3,000 episodes) on both datasets.

**[Insert TABLE 3 about here.]**

**TABLE 3.** OSR detection method comparison (6-way 5-shot, full_model, 3,000 episodes). Top: TAU22. Bottom: TAU19. ACC and Cls-AUROC fixed by configuration; OSR-AUROC and OSR Score determined by detection head. Bold = best OSR-AUROC.

On TAU22 (TABLE 3 top), anti-prototype [9] records AUROC = 0.624, OSR Score = 60.53%—better than random but far from usable. Mahalanobis [8] is effectively broken (AUROC = 0.486). The three OOD head methods (osr23, ext_v2, clust_v2) cluster tightly at AUROC ≈ 0.834–0.841 with OSR Score ≈ 73–74%; they are statistically indistinguishable (§IV.C). Fusion V2 reaches AUROC = 0.862 (+0.021 over the best single head). Gains are modest on TAU22, where uniform recording conditions compress the known/unknown margin.

TAU19 (TABLE 3 bottom) amplifies the gaps. Anti-prototype worsens to AUROC = 0.635; Mahalanobis jumps to 0.845 (+0.359). The OOD head family all exceed AUROC = 0.93; Fusion V2 reaches 0.972 with TPR@TNR95 = 90.22%. The fusion margin widens to +0.035–0.040.

Cls-AUROC and OSR-AUROC are orthogonal: TAU22 Cls-AUROC = 0.840 (higher than TAU19's 0.717), yet TAU19 OSR-AUROC dominates across all six heads. Classification and open-set detection draw on different properties of the feature representation. The OOD head family tracks within \(\pm 0.005\) AUROC; anti-prototype and Mahalanobis swing wildly—a pattern confirmed in §IV.C.

### C. Statistical Significance Test

We apply the Friedman test with Nemenyi post-hoc [12] across 72 conditions (6 OSR methods × 12 way-shot settings per dataset).

**[Insert TABLE 4 about here.]**

**TABLE 4.** Friedman test (\(\chi^2 = 49.29\), \(p = 1.94 \times 10^{-9}\), CD = 3.08 at \(\alpha = 0.05\)) with Nemenyi post-hoc. Average rankings (lower = better).

The method-level test is strongly significant: \(\chi^2 = 49.29\), \(p = 1.94 \times 10^{-9}\). Fusion V2 ranks best (1.75), followed by osr23 (2.0), cluster_v2 (2.5); anti-prototype and Mahalanobis tie last (5.5). Four pairwise differences survive Nemenyi correction (CD = 3.08): fusion_v2 > anti-prototype, fusion_v2 > Mahalanobis, osr23 > anti-prototype, osr23 > Mahalanobis. The OOD head family shows no significant pairwise differences, confirming functional interchangeability.

Testing the six model configurations over the same 72 conditions yields \(\chi^2 = 7.14\), \(p = 0.21\)—not significant. Architectural choices matter far less than OSR head choice. This underpins our central claim: the OSR head is the primary locus of performance variation.

### D. Complexity Comparison

TABLE 5 reports trainable parameters (excluding frozen YAMNet) and average inference time (AIT, ms/query, mean ± std over 1,000 forward passes on RTX 3090).

**[Insert TABLE 5 about here.]**

**TABLE 5.** Computational complexity. Params = trainable parameters excluding frozen YAMNet [11] backbone.

The Flow Transform [17], [18] dominates both axes. Removing it slashes parameters from 40.1K to 14.8K (−63%) and AIT from 4.16 ms to 0.65 ms (6.5× speedup). The OOD head adds ~700 parameters with negligible AIT overhead. The only_distance_head baseline requires 12.7K parameters and 0.64 ms/query.

For edge deployment, the 6.5× speedup from dropping the Flow Transform is decisive: 0.65 ms/query ≈ 1,500 queries/second on a single core. Combined with wo_flow_transform + fusion_v2 achieving the TAU22 optimum (AUROC = 0.884, §IV.F), an on-device system gets both best AUROC and fastest inference by removing the flow. On TAU19, the optimum (wo_ood_head + fusion_v2, AUROC = 0.985) retains the flow at 4.16 ms—a dataset-dependent accuracy/efficiency trade-off. SOTA methods (FEAT [4], MET [23], D-ProtoNet [22]) report comparable or larger parameter budgets but no open-set metrics, precluding direct FOAC efficiency comparison.

### E. Generalization Ability (Cross-Domain)

We evaluate bidirectional transfer: train on one dataset's base classes (200 epochs), test on the other's novel classes with target-domain unknowns.

**[Insert TABLE 6 about here.]**

**TABLE 6.** Cross-domain transfer. Classification accuracy and Cls-AUROC for Base/Novel 1-shot and 5-shot (top rows); OSR performance by method (bottom rows). Left: TAU22→TAU19. Right: TAU19→TAU22.

The dominant finding is asymmetry. TAU22→TAU19 with fusion_v2 attains OSR Score = 90.49%, AUROC = 0.977; TAU19→TAU22 peaks at OSR Score = 74.31%, AUROC = 0.878—a 16.2 pp and 0.099 gap. When TAU19 is the target, its per-device clusters create well-separated gaps in feature space; unknowns land in the gaps regardless of training domain. When TAU22 is the target, tighter, overlapping class distributions leave less open space.

Classification and OSR diverge under domain shift. Within-domain to cross-domain (TAU22→TAU19), Base 5-shot ACC drops 17 pp (52.27% → 35.13%), yet fusion_v2 OSR Score rises from 76.96% to 90.49%. Classification relies on fine between-class distinctions fragile under shift; open-set detection depends on compactness of the known-class manifold, which can improve even as between-class discrimination degrades.

Fusion V2 leads both directions: +37.7 pp OSR Score / +0.302 AUROC over anti-prototype on TAU22→TAU19; +23.4 pp / +0.253 over Mahalanobis on TAU19→TAU22. The OOD head family is stable under cross-domain evaluation (OSR Score ≈ 84%, AUROC ≈ 0.93 on TAU22→TAU19, within 1 pp); fusion_v2 adds ~6 pp. On TAU19→TAU22, all methods degrade ~10 pp—cross-domain toward the less diverse dataset is the hardest regime.

### F. Ablation Experiments

We ablate the full cross of 6 model configurations × 6 OSR heads = 36 conditions per dataset. The complete TAU19 AUROC matrix appears in TABLE 7; Fig 2 renders the same data as a heatmap where darker cells correspond to higher AUROC.

**[Insert Fig 2 about here.]**

**Fig 2.** Ablation heatmap: AUROC for 6 configurations (rows) × 6 OSR heads (columns) on TAU19. Darker = higher AUROC. The fusion_v2 column (rightmost) is consistently darkest; the wo_ood_head row achieves the global maximum at the fusion_v2 intersection.

**[Insert TABLE 7 about here.]**

**TABLE 7.** TAU19 ablation AUROC matrix. Bold + ★ = global optimum (wo_ood_head × fusion_v2, AUROC = 0.985). TAU22 ablation data (optimum: wo_flow_transform × fusion_v2, AUROC = 0.884) in Supplementary Material.

Fusion V2 achieves the highest AUROC in all 12 cells across both datasets. The global optimum is **wo_ood_head + fusion_v2 on TAU19: AUROC = 0.985**, OSR Score = 93.31%. This is the paper's most counterintuitive result: removing the OSR-specific training loss improves open-set detection. The hypothesis (§V.B) is that the OSR loss introduces noisy gradients interfering with natural feature organization; classification cross-entropy plus separation loss, with a well-structured head, suffices when features are strong.

On TAU22, the optimum is **wo_flow_transform + fusion_v2 (AUROC = 0.884)**. The Flow Transform [17], [18] helps when per-class distributions are roughly Gaussian but is neutral or mildly harmful on TAU19's multi-device distribution—don't pay the 6.5× latency cost if it doesn't improve AUROC.

Key patterns: reciprocal attention [9] is the largest ACC contributor (removing it drops TAU22 5s ACC −1.77 pp). The OOD head family produces AUROC within \(\pm 0.005\) across configurations. AUROC spread is wide on TAU19 (0.635 → 0.985, range = 0.354) but tighter on TAU22 (0.486 → 0.884, range = 0.398)—method and configuration choices matter most in challenging domains. The TAU22 ablation matrix is in Supplementary Material.

### G. Extended Experiments

#### G1. Way-Shot Analysis

We vary N = {5, 6} and K = {1, 5, 10} with Fusion V2.

**[Insert TABLE 8 about here.]**

**TABLE 8.** Way-shot scaling for Fusion V2 on TAU22 (top) and TAU19 (bottom). Best OSR Score in bold.

Increasing K from 1 to 10 boosts ACC by 10–15 pp; OSR metrics barely move (AUROC < +0.02, OSR Score < +5 pp). Classification and OSR are largely decoupled—more support samples improve class identification but don't substantially change known/unknown discrimination.

Increasing ways from 5 to 6 reduces ACC by 2–3 pp while OSR stays flat. Under the hardest condition (6-way 1-shot), Fusion V2 maintains AUROC = 0.976 (TAU19) / 0.874 (TAU22); anti-prototype collapses to 0.694 / 0.627. Fusion V2 wins all 12 way-shot settings with a consistent ~0.03–0.05 AUROC margin over the OOD head family.

One anomaly: Mahalanobis AUROC on TAU22 drops from 0.657 (5w1s) to 0.539 (5w10s). More support samples tighten Gaussian fits, perversely making the detector more brittle.

#### G2. Openness Analysis

We increase unknown classes from 2 to 4.

**[Insert TABLE 9 about here.]**

**TABLE 9.** Openness analysis. OSR Score and AUROC at open2/open4 with Δ. TAU22 (top), TAU19 (bottom).

AUROC degrades monotonically with more unknowns. Degradation is worse on TAU22: ext_v2 drops −0.116, osr23/clust_v2 −0.106; Fusion V2 drops −0.076 (smallest). On TAU19, Fusion V2 drops merely −0.008, maintaining 0.984 at open4. Anti-prototype on TAU22 open4 reaches AUROC = 0.599 (near random). Mahalanobis shows the worst cross-dataset consistency (AUROC ≈ 0.57–0.60 on TAU22 vs. 0.83 on TAU19).

TAU19's robustness stems from heterogeneous recording conditions producing compact known-class clusters with wide inter-cluster gaps, making the open-space boundary resistant to additional unknowns. On TAU22, uniform conditions produce overlapping clusters and a fragile boundary.

FOAC-AIFP's OSR Score hovers at ~50% (random-chance level) with AUROC ≈ 0.45–0.53 and TPR@TNR95 below 6%, despite ResNet18 being trained from scratch on target-domain base classes.
