## VI. Conclusion

We introduced a unified episodic FOAC framework and systematically compared six OSR detection heads—anti-prototype, Mahalanobis distance, OSR23, Extended V2, Cluster V2, and Fusion V2—under controlled conditions. Fusion V2 blends a discriminative boundary signal with a generative density estimate via a learned scalar weight. It won every setting we tested: 12/12 way-shot, 6/6 ablation configurations, 4/4 openness levels, both cross-domain directions. The Friedman test over 72 conditions confirms the result is not noise: \(\chi^2 = 49.29\), \(p = 1.94 \times 10^{-9}\) [12]; the configuration-level test shows architectural details don't matter nearly as much (\(p = 0.21\)).

The headline numbers: TAU19 wo_ood_head + Fusion V2 hits AUROC = 0.985 (global optimum) and holds AUROC = 0.984–0.992 across 2–4 unknown classes. TAU22 wo_flow_transform + Fusion V2 reaches AUROC = 0.884 while cutting inference time 6.5× (4.16 → 0.65 ms) and parameters 63% (40.1K → 14.8K) [17], [18].

Two findings strike us as having implications beyond this paper. The first is the wo_ood_head counterintuitive result: removing the OSR-specific training loss produces the best open-set detector, suggesting that with a sufficiently strong pretrained backbone, a clean classification objective plus a well-structured detection head outperforms a complicated multi-term loss [5], [6]. The second is metric-level: AUROC [14] discriminates methods more sharply than OSR Score (ablation spread 0.354 vs. 40.5 pp on the same data) and is free of threshold-calibration variance. We recommend AUROC as the primary FOAC evaluation metric going forward.

Several directions are open. The lightweight configuration (no flow, 12.7K parameters, 0.64 ms/query) is practical for on-device deployment and warrants testing on embedded hardware. Validation on a third dataset from a different acoustic domain—speech [31], musical instruments [30], or bioacoustics—would test whether the Fusion V2 advantage generalizes beyond DCASE environmental sound. Comparison with audio foundation models (CLAP [15], AST [16], PaSST [28]) is overdue; whether larger pretrained representations make the wo_ood_head effect stronger or erase it is an open empirical question. Finally, the near-saturation of AUROC with increasing shots lacks a theory. Understanding why K = 1 and K = 10 produce nearly indistinguishable open-set AUROC—while classification accuracy jumps 10–15 pp—may reveal something fundamental about the geometry of feature spaces under metric-based few-shot learning.
